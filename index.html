Research snapshot My work asks: can a phone or drone camera count, segment, and grade every fruit in a dense greenhouse canopy? I build ripeness datasets for blueberries and tomatoes, lightweight segmentation for crowded berries, and vision-language models that adapt to new greenhouses with few labels. For aerospace inspection, I work on BladeDelta: turning photos of jet engine blades into a 3D map of damage that shows where each dent is and how deep it goes, tested on blades never seen during development.

BladeDelta: Jet Engine Blade Inspection

Locates dents on a blade's 3D mesh from multi-view photos and measures their depth. An evidence test reports damage only where the cameras can see it, and says "not supported" otherwise. On a sealed test (240 cases, 30 blades never used in development), it cut false alarms from 32% to 10%, abstained on 85% of hidden dents, and measured found dents to a median depth error of 0.03 mm. Next: transferring it to six real blade CAD designs.

CervixGuard

An evidence-based review layer for cervical biopsy AI: each warning links to the exact tissue the model used, so uncertain or conflicting grades go to a human reviewer. More training slides lifted the grading baseline from 60.2% to 77.5% macro recall; the review guard is still being tested against confidence-based review.
